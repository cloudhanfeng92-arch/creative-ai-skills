# 90年代港式胶片武侠视觉语言

Use this as a stable grammar, not a bag of decorative keywords. Preserve the style DNA across a series while adapting every physical choice to the user's scene.

## STYLE_LOCK

- **Era and genre:** a still from a classic 1990s Hong Kong costume-wuxia film, with Jin-Yong-like jianghu emotion rather than palace pageantry, modern xianxia spectacle, or generic “ancient China.” For supernatural requests, extend this to restrained Hong Kong ghost-fantasy wuxia.
- **Photochemical image character:** 35mm vintage film, believable physical development, fine but visible grain, gentle tonal roll-off, mild Polaroid-like softness, matte haze, and soft edge halation. Grain must integrate with the image instead of looking like a uniform digital noise overlay.
- **Emotional register:** old, warm, restrained, lonely, mysterious, and fateful. Tenderness, reunion, waiting, departure, or resolve may enter, but performance stays controlled rather than melodramatic.
- **Spatial character:** layered foreground, middle ground, and background; asymmetric balance; useful negative space; a natural foreground obstruction; backgrounds compressed into soft color fields, bokeh, mist, rain, or silhouettes.
- **Material truth:** aged cotton-linen, coarse cloth, thin gauze, bamboo, hemp rope, weathered wood, matte stone, wet earth, old brick, tarnished metal, paper lanterns, and faded banners. Use only materials relevant to the scene and describe how wind, rain, weight, or light affects them.
- **Stillness with movement:** favor one suspended pre-action or emotional instant. Wind in hair, hat ties, hems and sleeves; drifting curtain, rain, smoke, dust, horse movement, or lantern sway supplies restrained life.

## Color grammar

Color is always a low-saturation warm/cool relationship, never a fixed palette pasted onto every scene.

- Useful bases: earth brown, warm brown, smoky cyan, blue-gray, cold gray, dark green, weathered black, faded brick red, muted indigo, and aged warm yellow.
- Warm gold, warm orange, candle amber, or old yellow should usually have a specific job: sunset, lamp, skin warmth, rim light, window glow, or one small narrative accent.
- Smoky cyan, cool blue, and gray-blue belong naturally in shade, rain, night, mist, distance, or stone reflections.
- Red, green, blue, white, and black garments or architecture must be described as faded, weathered, or integrated into the environment. Avoid pure black, candy color, neon cyan-purple, and modern teal-orange grading.
- In every prompt, map colors to concrete objects and explain the visual job of the warm/cool contrast.

## Lighting grammar

Name one dominant source, its direction, what it reveals, the atmospheric medium, and at most one or two motivated secondary effects. Highlights remain soft and unblown; shadows retain film texture.

| Scene condition | Useful motivated treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Forest, courtyard, garden at dusk | Warm side-backlight or one-sided rim light through leaves or architecture; very soft frontal fill; mist or dust catches sparse beams. |
| Interior at night | Warm lamp/candle side light on face, hands, table, and prop; cool window or moon rim on hair and shoulder; curtain or smoke softens the transition. |
| Rain or temple steps | Broad cool side/back daylight defines rain lines and wet reflections; a small warm interior accent may add depth; face receives only gentle fill. |
| Moonlit roof, bridge, mountain, or town | Cool moon side/backlight outlines hair, robe, weapon, rope, or eaves; distant aged warm lamps form restrained halos through night mist. |
| Wall, battlefield, or imminent action | Slightly harder cool side light and a clearer light-shadow boundary; minimal frontal fill; dust, smoke, hair, bowstring, or blade edge catches the light. |
| Snow or overcast day | Large cool diffuse source, pale reflected fill from snow or sky, restrained warm skin or distant shelter light; never reuse sunset shafts without a physical cause. |
| Dawn or water | Soft oblique dawn light with cool ambient fill; water, fog, or pale wall provides a believable secondary reflection. |

Do not use arbitrary god rays, every-direction rim light, glossy studio key light, clipped highlights, crushed blacks, or flat global illumination.

## Camera and composition

- Default to a mid-telephoto lens, large aperture, and shallow or very shallow depth of field for portraits and relational scenes. Use medium-close, half-body, or medium framing most often.
- Let bamboo leaves, wet eaves, another character's shoulder, hair, flowers, rope, old brick, a flag, smoke, or rain occupy a soft foreground layer when appropriate.
- Place the main subject slightly off-center and retain calm negative space. Use stairs, bridge ropes, a zither, bow and arrow, roofline, or path as an honest leading line.
- A two-person emotional exchange often benefits from an over-the-shoulder composition with one soft foreground figure and the other face and gaze in focus.
- Environment, cavalry, or large supernatural scenes may widen to a compressed medium-long or long view. Preserve clear silhouettes, simple scale relationships, and large quiet areas rather than modern ultra-wide distortion.
- Vary shot scale and spatial device across a series. Do not produce near-identical portraits with nouns swapped.

## People, costume, and performance

- Describe each principal figure as identity and life experience, then hair affected by weather, facial structure and eyes, gaze, and a restrained inner state.
- Useful emotional tones include calm vigilance, fatigue, distance, quiet tenderness, hesitation, resolve, grief held back, and pre-battle concentration. Avoid exaggerated shouting, model poses, and direct-to-camera glamour unless requested.
- Use loose ancient Chinese martial robes, long gowns, cross-collar layers, skirts, cloaks, or riding garments in coarse cotton-linen, soft woven cloth, or thin gauze. Show natural weight, relaxed folds, localized wetness, abrasion, dust, and age.
- Avoid modern tailoring, luxury embroidery by default, plastic sheen, pristine costume-rental styling, heavy beauty makeup, airbrushed skin, ornate palace crowns, and fashion-editorial posing.
- Bind every prop to an owner and action. State which hand holds, draws, presses, steadies, or releases it; keep one primary action per person.

## Scene and story hook

Give each image a clear place, time, and weather; legible foreground/middle/background anchors; and one quiet narrative hook. A blurred distant figure, half-open window, untaken seat, extinguishing lamp, abandoned scabbard, faraway settlement lights, unseen arrival, or partially concealed creature can imply story without clutter.

Environment serves the relationship and action. Do not pile on temples, lanterns, bamboo, mountains, crowds, and fog merely to look “Chinese.”

## Supernatural and action adaptation

- Supernatural creatures or phenomena should feel like 1990s optical, miniature, wire-work, matte-painting, or practical effects: partly concealed by cloud, mist, darkness, or silhouette; tactile and dreamlike; never hyper-sharp glossy CGI.
- Action should be a readable frozen instant with one clear contact chain, strong line of force, and cloth/hair/environmental response. Avoid modern superhero posing, exploding energy circles, excessive particles, and video-game VFX unless explicitly requested.

## Anti-style boundary

Exclude sharp digital edges, HDR, oversharpening, modern commercial or fashion photography, glossy 3D rendering, generic anime, ink-wash illustration, neon cyan-purple, high-saturation digital effects, plastic skin, immaculate cosplay, random text, captions, borders, logos, signatures, and fake watermarks. A platform-required AI mark is not a visual defect and must not be evaded.
